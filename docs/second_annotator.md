# Second annotator — instructions

*Kurz auf Deutsch:* Bitte ordnen Sie jede der 50 Firmenbeschreibungen genau
einem NACE-Abschnitt (A–U) zu. Etwa 30 Minuten. Die Regeln stehen in
[`annotation_guidelines.md`](annotation_guidelines.md), Abschnitte 2–4.

## Why this is needed

The evaluation labels were produced by a language model and checked by one
person, the author, who does not read German and judged each document from an
English machine translation. That gives a human-versus-model agreement figure.
What reviewers ask for first is human-versus-human agreement on the original
text, and only someone who reads German can provide it.

## Who should do it

- Reads German fluently, including company-register phrasing
  (*Gegenstand des Unternehmens ist …*).
- Has not seen the project's labels, the model's suggestions or the author's
  answers. Independence is the whole point.

## What to do

1. Get `results/second_annotator_sample.csv` (generated by
   `make second-annotator`). It holds 50 company purpose statements in German,
   shuffled, with no translation and no suggestion.
2. Read [`annotation_guidelines.md`](annotation_guidelines.md) sections 2–4.
   The three boundary rules in section 4 matter most: holding shells,
   IT versus consulting, and waste containers.
3. For each row, write one NACE section letter (A–U) in `true_sector`. If you
   are unsure, still choose the best letter and say why in `annotator_note`.
   You can use `tools/annotate.html` (open it in a browser and load the CSV) or
   any spreadsheet program.
4. Do not open `results/verification_sample.csv`, `results/annotation_queue.csv`
   or `data/evaluation/human_labels.json` before you finish; they contain the
   other answers.
5. Return the file. The author puts it back at
   `results/second_annotator_sample.csv` and runs `make second-annotator-score`,
   which writes `results/second_annotator_report.json`.

## What happens with the result

Both figures are reported: agreement with the author (human versus human) and
agreement with the model's labels. A low number is reported as it is. If the
paper is accepted, the second annotator is thanked by name in the camera-ready
version, with their permission.
